# Video script (≈2:45), timed to the 11 site scenes

**[→]** = press the right arrow. Record in Chrome, fullscreen, webcam over the dashed circle.

**1 · Intro (0:00–0:15).** Hold 2 s while the title fades in.
> "Hi, we're team [name] from BMSIT&M. This is DrishtiSaathi, which means 'companion for your vision', built for the AI-Powered Digital Experiences stream."

**[→] 2 · The problem (0:15–0:30).** Let the text finish focusing before speaking.
> "Every cataract patient has to choose a lens that stays in their eye for life. Most choose in a few minutes, from an English brochure, in a language they don't speak at home."

**[→] 3 · Where it breaks (0:30–0:50)**
> "Cataract is the leading cause of blindness in India. Counselling is rushed. The brochures are full of jargon. And after surgery, clinics are buried in repeat calls while real warning signs wait in the queue. So patients pick on price, or regret a lens they paid more for."

**[→] 4 · Four lens types (0:50–1:05).** The cards rotate on their own; point at the chart.
> "There are four main lens types, and each one sees differently. This chart shows it: a monofocal is sharp only far away, while a trifocal stays sharp all the way to reading distance, at the cost of more night halos."

**[→] 5 · Our solution (1:05–1:25).** The chat plays on the phone; tap the mic button to replay.
> "DrishtiSaathi is a voice-first assistant. Here, Ramesh, a tailor, tells it in Kannada that he threads needles all day and rides his scooter at night. It understands that both near and night vision matter to him, and prepares a lens shortlist for his surgeon to approve."

**[→] 6 · How it helps (1:25–1:40)**
> "One assistant does four jobs. It speaks the patient's language. It ranks lenses using their eye measurements and daily life. It answers questions only from the clinic's own guidance. And after surgery it sends drop reminders and alerts the doctor immediately if something's wrong."

**[→] 7 · See before you choose (1:40–2:00).** Drag the slider slowly, then click Monofocal, then Trifocal.
> "This is the moment no brochure can give. On the left is a cataract today. On the right is the same street after surgery. With a monofocal, the road is sharp but the medicine card is blurry. With a trifocal, everything is sharp, but you can see the halos around the lights at night. Patients see the trade-off before they choose."

**[→] 8 · Expected impact (2:00–2:15)**
> "The impact: patients choose on their daily life, not on price. Surgeons review a ready shortlist instead of repeating the same explanation. Clinics get fewer repeat calls, and warning signs reach a doctor right away."

**[→] 9 · How we build it (2:15–2:30)**
> "We'll build it on Microsoft Azure. Azure AI Speech handles Indian languages, a FastAPI orchestrator runs grounded RAG on Azure OpenAI, an explainable model ranks the lenses, and every recommendation goes to a surgeon dashboard. It never diagnoses, and every answer cites its source."

**[→] 10 · Build plan (2:30–2:40)**
> "We'll build it in four phases: the knowledge base, the voice assistant, the recommendation engine and simulator, and finally surgeon testing."

**[→] 11 · Thank you (2:40–2:48)**
> "DrishtiSaathi helps every patient see the choice clearly, before they see the world clearly again. Thank you."

## Tips
- Type the team name into scenes 1 and 11 before recording.
- Do one silent run to learn the → timing.
- Over 3 minutes? Trim scenes 3 and 6 first. Keep scene 7, the simulator; it's the strongest moment.
